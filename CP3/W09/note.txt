Problem A:

Each cycle can be thought of as the number of connected components.

You can trivially calculate the number of ways to break a cycle into x components. Doing that, you get m polynomials,
where m is the number of cycles.

p[x][i] : number of ways to break a cycle x into i components

p[x][i] = nCk(len[x], len[x] - i);

To combine these polynomials, you can maintain a priority_queue of polynomial lengths, and then repeatedly
combine 2 polynomial of smallest lengths, resulting in a O(n*log^2(n)) time complexity (?).

poly[i] : Final resulting polynomial

After we got the final polynomial, we can multiply the number of arrangements of the components, and
perform a PIE to get the final answer.

arr[i] : number of ways to construct a permutation with at most i components.

arr[i] = fac[i] * poly[i];
